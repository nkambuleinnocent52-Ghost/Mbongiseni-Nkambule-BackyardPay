# Tenant reporting and security deposit: research-to-design decision

7 November 2026. The owner asked for research before implementing tenant problem reporting alongside property-level security deposits.

## What the evidence supports

A report should be an account of an issue, not an automatic allocation of repair liability. Rental Housing Act 50 of 1999 s. 4(5)(d)(i) distinguishes fair wear and tear from the duty to return property in good repair, and s. 4(5)(e) addresses compensation for damage caused by a tenant, household member or visitor. The cause of a broken window cannot be assumed merely from its location. A tenant should still report damage they may have caused so inspection, safety and repair can be arranged. Recording it does not by itself admit a legally established debt.

A leak, failed gate or shared-facility defect should be reported to the landlord/manager promptly. Whether the landlord ultimately bears the cost depends on cause, agreed responsibilities and applicable law; a problem in municipal infrastructure may require the relevant municipal service. The app must not promise that every outside repair is the landlord's financial responsibility. For immediate danger or serious ongoing damage, users need direct emergency/municipal contact and landlord contact; the app is not an emergency dispatch service.

Noise/nuisance complaints can be recorded factually for landlord follow-up. Immediate threats, crimes or public-service issues may require police/municipal services rather than waiting for a landlord's app response. Avoid unnecessary personal allegations or broadcasting one tenant's complaint to every neighbour.

The Eastern Cape Rental Housing Tribunal's official page states that tenants and landlords can lodge rental complaints and describes investigation, mediation and hearings. It is a dispute/escalation route, not proof that a report automatically determines damages or authorises entry or eviction. The current page was successfully retrieved and read on 7 November 2026: https://www.ecdhs.gov.za/index.php/tribunal/ .

The Act's s. 4(2) addresses reasonable inspection after reasonable notice. Reporting an issue should not be interpreted as blanket permission to enter; arrange access separately. Sections 5(3)(e)-(j) address condition inspections, deposits, supported costs, refund situations and the consequences of inspection failures. A reasonable supported R400 window deduction does not automatically forfeit a whole R2,000 deposit. Other established liabilities and interest also belong in an itemised reconciliation. No fixed deposit interest rate or blanket 30-day forfeiture rule is justified.

## Implemented workflow

- Private report categories: water/plumbing, gate/shared facilities, electrical/safety, structure/damp, noise/nuisance, damage inside the room, account/deposit query and other.
- Report includes urgency, actual date noticed, exact location, factual description, what the reporter knows about cause, and optional availability for an agreed visit. Unknown cause is valid. Self-caused damage is allowed.
- Both parties can add messages; the landlord maintains open/in-progress/resolved workflow status. The tenant can comment if the issue remains unresolved. Workflow status does not adjudicate responsibility.
- Photos/PDF evidence use existing private rental storage and are linked to the report. Existing documents remain intact. Another landlord or household cannot access the report through the app's scope rules.
- Landlord has an overview of tenant reports, their property/room, urgency and state. Existing in-app notices notify the relevant account. Automatic SMS, contractor dispatch and background push are not implemented.
- No report creates rent charges, deductions or a liability finding. Deposit events remain explicit landlord-entered financial records with existing inspection/evidence prerequisites and a balance limit.

## Deposit and onboarding

Each property has an explicitly entered security deposit; enter 0 where no deposit is agreed. Existing properties initially have an unspecified setting, rather than an invented R2,000 amount. Complete it before a new tenant is onboarded. The setting snapshots into each new tenancy, including signup into a vacant room, but is not a receipt of payment. Changes to the property default do not rewrite older agreements or money records.

At onboarding, show security deposit, first-month rent and total initially due. With R2,000 deposit, R1,500 monthly rent and 15 January move-in, the total is R2,822.58: R2,000 security plus R822.58 rent. On the tenant's account, show the remaining deposit and remaining rent; an amount already recorded as received is not demanded again. The refundable deposit balance, including interest/refund/deduction events, stays separate from rent income. The combined overview total is presentation, not a bank transfer or duplicate charge.

If a single bank transfer covers both purposes, users record the rent component and deposit component separately against the same actual bank reference. The forms explain that the full transfer must not be entered twice. This release does not automatically import, bank-verify or split bank transactions, and does not manufacture two independent bank transfers.
