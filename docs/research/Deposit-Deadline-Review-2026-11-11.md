# Initial security-deposit deadline — research review

11 November 2026. South African rental context, with Eastern Cape dispute-service information. This is a research note; no new East London participant findings are claimed.

## Answer

There is no general initial-deposit grace period of 7, 14 or 30 days after a tenant moves in in the national Rental Housing Act provisions reviewed. Section 5(3)(c) permits a landlord to require payment **before moving into the dwelling**, in the amount specified in the agreement or otherwise agreed between the parties. A deposit is not automatically compulsory for every rental: the provision says the landlord **may require** it, and s. 5(6) includes the deposit amount **if any** among the written particulars.

Therefore the payment deadline depends on the actual agreement and any applicable rules. If the agreed condition is payment before occupation, the tenant has no automatic statutory right under this provision to postpone it by a month after moving in. A landlord can agree to a later date or an instalment arrangement; record the exact agreed amounts and dates in writing. That is an agreed concession, not a universal legal grace period. If occupation has already begun without a clear deadline, establish and record the agreement instead of inventing an automatic app deadline.

A practical onboarding policy can be “deposit due before move-in, unless the landlord agrees to specified later payments.” This is a recommended arrangement to agree, not a statutory deposit required for every property. Adding a tenant or allowing occupation must not falsely mark their deposit as paid.

The landlord can follow up on an overdue agreed deposit and use lawful contractual/dispute remedies. Nonpayment does not authorise an app-triggered eviction, lockout, seizure or utility disconnection. Section 4(5) addresses agreed charges, recovery through lawful proceedings and repossession after a court order. The 2007 amendment adds unlawful lockout/utilities shutoff to the Act's offences. Any cancellation or breach notice must follow the applicable agreement and law; no universal notice period is inferred here.

## Application implications

- Keep deposit required, actual received, pending reports and outstanding separate from rent.
- A tenant can report “I paid the deposit”; only the landlord's checked confirmation records receipt. A pending submission does not settle the deposit.
- If the landlord agrees to defer or accept instalments, store an actual agreed deadline/payment plan rather than a hardcoded 30-day allowance.
- A late indicator/reminder must not remove a tenant's records or automatically end occupation.
- The deposit-return periods of 7/14/21 days concern different end-of-tenancy circumstances. They are not grace periods for initially paying a deposit.

## Short explanation for the user

“Usually, agree to receive the deposit before the tenant moves in. South African law does not give every tenant an automatic month to pay it afterwards. If the landlord agrees to let them pay later or in instalments, use those agreed dates. In the app, their deposit remains outstanding until the landlord confirms the money was actually received.”
